About FinanceHub

FinanceHub is an independent personal finance blog for U.S. readers. It publishes practical, plain-English guides on budgeting, saving, small business money management, AI money tools and beginner investing.

Who writes FinanceHub

My name is William Lima. I am a software developer, not a financial advisor, and I created FinanceHub because I wanted a place to share the money frameworks I actually use and study: a budget I can maintain in a few minutes a week, sinking funds for predictable bills, an emergency fund built in stages, and a calm, long-term view of investing.

Working in software taught me to break big, messy problems into small steps, check assumptions against reliable documentation and test whether a system still works when real life gets in the way. I apply the same habits to personal finance. Most money problems are not solved by a clever trick. They are solved by a clear system that a busy person can follow on an ordinary month.

I write and edit every article on FinanceHub. When a topic requires rules that change, such as tax brackets, retirement account limits or credit reporting rules, I rely on primary sources rather than my own opinion.

What I am, and what I am not

I want to be direct about my background so you can judge the content fairly.

  • I am not a Certified Financial Planner, CPA, attorney, licensed investment adviser or tax professional.
  • I do not provide personalized financial, investment, tax or legal advice, and nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy or sell a specific security.
  • I am a developer with a genuine, long-standing interest in personal finance who researches carefully and organizes reliable information for everyday readers.
  • When your situation involves significant tax, legal or investment decisions, I encourage you to talk with a qualified professional.

How articles are researched

Every guide starts with a practical question a real reader might have, such as "How much should I keep in an emergency fund?" or "What does my W-4 actually change?" I then check the topic against primary and official sources before writing.

  • Government and regulatory sources such as IRS.gov, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), Investor.gov from the SEC, FINRA, the FDIC and the Federal Trade Commission.
  • Official plan, account or product documentation when an article explains how a type of account works.
  • Worked examples with clearly hypothetical numbers, so you can see how a framework behaves before applying it to your own budget.

Figures that change every year, such as contribution limits or tax thresholds, are described as subject to change, and I encourage readers to confirm current numbers with the official source.

What FinanceHub covers

  • Personal finance fundamentals: budgeting, credit, paychecks and monthly money reviews.
  • Saving and budgeting habits: emergency funds, sinking funds, automated savings and debt payoff.
  • Small business cash flow, pricing, reserves and financial statements.
  • AI and fintech tools, including how to use them safely and how to recognize new types of fraud.
  • Beginner investing concepts such as stocks, ETFs, retirement accounts and diversification.

Editorial standards

FinanceHub avoids get-rich-quick promises, unsupported claims and clickbait. Articles explain frameworks and tradeoffs so you can adapt them to your own circumstances, rather than telling you what to do with your money.

Each article shows its publication date and the date it was last updated. When I find an error or a rule changes, I update the article and its date. Advertising may appear on this site, but advertisers do not review, approve or influence what I write.

Corrections and contact

If you spot an error, an outdated number or a topic you would like explained, please reach out through the contact page. I read every message, and reader questions often become future guides.
